What do “Coverage Lens”, “Event Lens”, and “Amplification Gap” mean?

Coverage Lens = news volume. Every article counts.

Event Lens = unique developments. Repeated reporting about the same event counts once.

Amplification Gap = the difference between the two. It tells us whether repetition makes the news picture look more empowering or more constraining.

What does “non-empowerment” actually contain?

“Non-empowerment” does not mean “AI has no impact” or “the story is unimportant.” It means the available headline/snippet does not establish a direct change in human capability, autonomy, rights, protection, participation, or influence.

Six ideas for reading the signal

Use the index as a guide, not a league table

  1. Investment is not empowerment. More capital and infrastructure do not automatically mean more human control.
  2. Capability grows unevenly. AI may strengthen organisations without reaching citizens equally.
  3. Creativity has two sides. New tools can coexist with reduced consent or compensation.
  4. Agency asks who decides. Productivity gains can happen while people lose decision authority.
  5. Rules redistribute power. Transparency, appeal, consultation, and enforcement affect who can influence AI.
  6. Read the evidence, not only the score. The Observatory summarises coded developments; it is not a country ranking.
